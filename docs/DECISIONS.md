| D-34 | Drop the Kaggle UCF frame mirror as the P1C/P2A pixel source and derive frames directly from the official UCF-Crime MP4 corpus. Extract **once** at native 320x240 into a JPEG-payload packed blob, and treat the model's training resolution as a separate registered decision resolved in the loader by downscaling, not baked into the extraction. | The Kaggle mirror is not temporally incomplete: sampled against the official MP4s on 2026-08-25, 15 of 15 videos showed exactly `ceil(n/10)` frames at indices 0,10,20,... i.e. 100% coverage of every 10th frame. Its real deficits are video-level and structural: it carries 1,610 of the official 1,900 source videos, is fixed at 64x64 against a 320x240 source, and is fixed at ~3 fps effective density. Deriving frames from the official corpus removes all three at once and additionally dissolves the substantive part of blocker B-001: that blocker exists because the mirror's frame-index-to-timestamp mapping was inherited and unverified against pixel content, and a self-performed extraction *defines* that mapping rather than having to reconstruct it. It also unblocks B-002, which required original-resolution frames that 64x64 could not supply. The I/O regime improves rather than degrades: NFS cost scales with file count, not bytes, and the corpus is 1,950 files averaging 51.3 MiB (median 14.3 MiB, p99 725 MiB, max 8,272 MiB) totalling 97.7 GiB, so a full sequential read is bandwidth-bound at roughly 3 minutes rather than latency-bound over 1.27M small files. Decode is not a bottleneck either: measured 3,667 frames/s per core for 320x240 H.264 demux+decode, so one full-corpus decode is about 63 core-minutes. Extraction is separated from training resolution because resolution, not I/O, is now the dominant compute cost: ResNet50 at 224x224 is 12.25x the FLOPs of 64x64, which projects a three-seed run from the 64x64 range into a multi-day range on one `mig-1g.18gb` slice, while GPU memory stays comfortable (an estimated ~5.5 GiB of 16 GiB usable at batch 64). A JPEG payload rather than a raw one is chosen for the same reason: raw uint8 at native resolution is ~318 GB and would not survive per-pod staging, whereas a JPEG payload is roughly 10x smaller, and at any resolution above 64x64 the accelerator, not the loader, is the binding constraint, so spending worker CPU on decode is free. D-33's blob format, `numpy.memmap` loader, fail-closed digest semantics, runner wiring, and per-pod emptyDir staging design are all source-agnostic and carry over unchanged; only the packer's input stage is replaced. Numbering note: D-32 is intentionally reserved for the deferred CUDA precision-policy decision and is not a gap in the record. |
| D-35 | Fix the D-34 extraction temporal stride at **5** (every 5th decoded frame, ~6 fps at the corpus's nominal 30 fps), and treat stride as the one extraction parameter that is deliberately over-provisioned relative to current need. | Stride and resolution are not symmetric in cost-to-revise, and that asymmetry, not a scientific preference for 6 fps, drives this. Resolution is cheap to change after the fact: the blob is extracted at native 320x240 and any experiment downscales in the loader, so a resolution change costs nothing but loader configuration. Stride is expensive to change after the fact: a denser stride cannot be recovered from a sparser blob and requires re-decoding the full 97.7 GiB corpus. Stride 5 is therefore chosen over stride 10 as a hedge, not as a claim that 6 fps is required. It doubles the temporal resolution available to the deferred temporal-baseline work (B-006), which is the most likely consumer to need density the current frame-level protocol does not, at a cost that remains operationally trivial: roughly 2.8M frames and ~64 GB of JPEG payload against 65 TB free on `/home`, staging to a pod's node-local emptyDir in about two minutes at the probed 562 MiB/s. Stride 1 was rejected: it is ~13.8M frames and ~318 GB, which pushes per-pod staging to roughly ten minutes and buys density no registered or planned work item has asked for. Consequence for comparability: the extracted blob is a superset of the Kaggle mirror's every-10th-frame sampling, so a stride-10 view is recoverable from it by index arithmetic alone; any experiment intending to reproduce the mirror's temporal density must select that subset explicitly in its manifest rather than relying on the blob's native density, and must say so in its config. |

| D-41 | The registered P1C training point is **224×224, batch 64, bfloat16, throughput policy V5** (channels_last + torch.compile + fused AdamW). | Owner decisions on 2026-08-27, each on measured evidence. **224×224** over native 240×320: native costs 1.49× the wall clock (268.1 vs 400.5 img/s) for 1.53× the pixels, and 224 matches the geometry the ImageNet backbone was pretrained at, giving a 7×7 pre-GAP map against native's 8×10 (P1C-VIDEO-SWEEP-009). **Batch 64** over 128/256: at this resolution batch size has stopped being a throughput lever — 64→256 buys only 1.09× — while 64 gives twice the optimizer steps of 128 and half the device memory; since `lr` is not scaled with batch these are different experiments, not the same one at different speeds. **bfloat16** over float16: 1.24× for zero skipped optimizer steps and no scaler state, where fp16 bought a further 0.18× in exchange for a loss-scaling mechanism that discarded 2 of 13 steps (P1C-VIDEO-PRECISION-CALIBRATION-007/008). **Throughput V5**: 1.745×, 403.2 → 703.7 img/s, zero skipped steps, no change to the arithmetic (P1C-VIDEO-SWEEP-010). Net effect: the four-fold three-seed protocol drops from ~3.2–3.8 days to ~1.8–2.2 days on the single slice. Consequence and constraint: the throughput options change which CUDA kernels run, so a run is deterministic within a policy but not bitwise comparable across policies — every arm of a RESULT 1 / RESULT 2 comparison must hold `training.throughput` fixed. Supersedes D-37's 64×64 choice, which was made when the wall-clock cost of 224×224 was estimated rather than measured and before the throughput work existed. |
